### Behavioral method: causal choice under constraints and changed evidence
Select an executable product decision, not a narrative about good practice.
1. Extract the objective, factual authority, current state, candidate options, must-preserve constraints and material unknowns. Reject any option violating a hard customer/legal/truth/safety constraint before ranking options by benefit.
2. When numbers are given, calculate comparable expected benefit and loss transparently (for example weighted outcome probabilities or expected complaints), label assumptions separately, and report sensitivity to the key uncertainty. Do not present invented probability as measured fact.
3. If an option seems beneficial but causal attribution or sample adequacy is unknown, prefer a reversible bounded pilot with a stated control, comparison metric, guardrail, stop condition and rollback rather than a broad rollout.
4. Make a selected decision and specific acceptance checks that distinguish the chosen option from the rejected alternative; preserve material debt, ownership, discount, eligibility and payment qualifiers.
5. When a newly verified source contradicts the previous decision, identify what changed, revise the decision and downstream handoff, stop unsupported claims, and keep valid unaffected commitments.
6. When enough material authority is supplied, decide without sending a redundant missing-input question; when the central truth cannot be resolved, emit the precise bounded block.
